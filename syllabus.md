## Course Description

Advanced Calculus I provides a rigorous treatment of the calculus of real-valued functions of several variables from the standpoint of modern mathematics. The course is a natural continuation of Calculus III and emphasizes both computational techniques and mathematical reasoning.

Topics include vectors and vector-valued functions, functions of several variables, partial and directional derivatives, differentiability, the gradient and chain rule, tangent planes, constrained and unconstrained optimization, higher derivatives, Taylor's formula, quadratic forms, potential functions, curve integrals, double integrals, and Green's Theorem.

Particular emphasis is placed on understanding definitions and theorems, constructing and interpreting mathematical arguments, reading and writing proofs, and connecting rigorous mathematical theory with computational methods and applications.

## Assessment and Evaluation

The course uses regular practice and assessment to develop both computational proficiency and theoretical understanding. There will be approximately **12 homework assignments and 12 quizzes**, two examinations, a comprehensive final examination, and online class participation.

### Homework

Approximately 12 homework assignments will be completed through **Pearson MyLab Math**. Homework is designed to reinforce the computational techniques and concepts developed in the course and to prepare students for quizzes and examinations.

Students are expected to complete each assignment carefully and to understand the mathematical reasoning behind their solutions rather than simply obtaining correct answers. Some theoretical or proof-based exercises may be assigned separately through Canvas when appropriate.

### Quizzes

Approximately 12 online quizzes will be administered through **Pearson MyLab Math**. Each quiz will normally correspond to the material covered by the associated homework assignment.

Homework provides guided practice, while quizzes evaluate each student's independent understanding of the material. Students should therefore be able to solve representative homework problems without relying on step-by-step assistance before attempting a quiz.

### Examinations

There will be three major examinations:

- **Exam 1**: approximately Week 7;
- **Exam 2**: approximately Week 12; and
- **Final Exam**: comprehensive and administered during the University's final examination period.

Examinations will be administered online through **MyLab Math** unless otherwise announced. Because Advanced Calculus I includes theoretical reasoning and proofs, an examination may also include written work or proof-based questions submitted through Canvas when specified by the instructor.

Exact examination dates, coverage, time limits, and submission instructions will be posted in Canvas.

Reading mathematical material is an essential part of Advanced Calculus. Students are expected to develop increasing independence in reading definitions, theorems, examples, and proofs.

Effective preparation includes:

- identifying the principal definitions, concepts, and notation;
- understanding the hypotheses and conclusions of important theorems;
- following and explaining mathematical proofs;
- working through representative examples independently;
- connecting geometric, algebraic, and analytic interpretations;
- practicing computational problems in MyLab Math; and
- attempting theoretical and proof-based exercises from Lang and supplemental course materials.

Students are encouraged to keep organized notes that summarize major definitions, theorems, examples, proof strategies, and questions that arise while reading.

## Course Content

The course covers the following required topics, with emphasis on both theoretical understanding and computational proficiency.

**I. Vectors.** Points in space; located vectors; scalar product; norm of a vector; parametric lines; planes; cross product.

**II. Differentiation of Vectors.** Derivatives of vector-valued functions; length of curves.

**III. Functions of Several Variables.** Graphs and level curves; partial derivatives; differentiability and gradient; repeated partial derivatives.

**IV. Chain Rule and Gradient.** Chain rule; tangent planes; directional derivatives; functions depending only on distance from the origin; conservation of energy; further techniques in partial differentiation.

**V. Maximum and Minimum.** Critical points; boundary points; Lagrange multipliers.

**VI. Higher Derivatives.** First two terms in Taylor's formula; quadratic term at critical points; algebraic study of quadratic forms; partial differential operators; general Taylor formula.

**VII. Potential Functions.** Existence and uniqueness of potential functions; local existence; special vector fields; differentiation under the integral; proof of the local existence theorem.

**VIII. Curve Integrals.** Definition and evaluation; reverse paths; integrals for vector fields with potential functions; path dependence and independence.

**IX. Double Integrals.** Double integrals; repeated integrals; polar coordinates.

**X. Green's Theorem.** Standard form of Green's Theorem; divergence and rotation of a vector field.
