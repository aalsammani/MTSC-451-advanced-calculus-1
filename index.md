Advanced Calculus I provides a rigorous treatment of the calculus of real-valued functions of several variables from the standpoint of modern mathematics. It continues Calculus III and emphasizes both computational techniques and mathematical reasoning: understanding definitions and theorems, reading and writing proofs, and connecting rigorous theory with computation and applications.

These are the lecture notes for the course. The theoretical development follows Serge Lang, *Calculus of Several Variables*; computational practice follows Briggs, Cochran, Gillett, and Schulz, *Calculus*, through MyLab Math. Announcements, assignments, and grades are in Canvas.

## How to Use These Notes

- **Before class**, read the assigned sections listed at the top of each lecture under *Reading*.
- **Definitions, theorems, and examples are numbered by lecture** (Definition 3.2 is the second definition in Lecture 03), so they can be cited in homework and discussion.
- **Concept Checks and In-Class Practice** hide their answers. Work each problem first, then open the solution to check your reasoning.
- **Interactive figures** let you change vectors and curves and watch the quantities in the lecture respond. They run in the browser; drag to move or rotate, or use the keyboard.
- **A PDF version** of every lecture is linked at the top of its page, for printing or offline reading.
- Use the search box (Ctrl + K) to find a definition or theorem by name.
